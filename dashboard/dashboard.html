Definitions & Method
Read the numbers correctly

Cohort — customers grouped by first-purchase month (Jul 2023 – Dec 2024 shown, 18 cohorts).

Purchase — an order with status Delivered or Returned. Cancelled orders never transacted; Processing is not yet a purchase.

Retention Mk — share of the cohort with at least one purchase k months after their first purchase. M0 = 100% by definition.

Reading — newer cohorts (bottom rows) retain visibly better than older ones: month-1 retention rises from ~15–28% (2023 cohorts) to 55–88% (late 2024). Retention improves as the business matures. Full SQL: sql/09_cohort_retention.sql · definitions: data/metrics.json.

Pricing Lab -- Q&A
Q1 Does the discount ladder pay for itself?
Answer: No. The Kruskal-Wallis test across discount depths gives p=0.305, meaning the volume response is not statistically significant. Every rung erodes margin without a detected volume lift.
Q2 What would it take for a 20% discount to break even?
Answer: Volume would need to rise +59.2% just to hold total margin flat. The current data shows no evidence of such a lift.
Q3 How much margin could be recovered by capping discounts at 10%?
Answer: ₹6.45 L (2.0% of total margin) across 3,294 line items currently above 10% discount. This is an estimated counterfactual, not realised savings.
Q4 What should we do?
Answer: Cap discounts at 10% as a policy change, then run a controlled test on any deeper discounts before scaling. The break-even simulator lets you stress-test any depth + lift combination.

Win-Back Economics -- Q&A
Q1 Who is the win-back audience?
Answer: The 441 cooling-off customers in the Medium + High churn-risk band (predicted churn probability ≥ 12.5%), holding ₹1.30 Cr of historical spend. These are modelled predictions, not observed churn.
Q2 What is the expected value per contact?
Answer: ₹523 at 15% assumed recovery (Rs 250 contact cost, Rs 13,292 retained value). This is expected net revenue before cost of goods -- at 54% gross margin it translates to roughly Rs 167 of contribution per contact.
Q3 Which scenario gives the best ROI?
Answer: Contacting the top decile by expected value (44 customers) yields ~Rs 24K expected net revenue for 10x less contact spend than the blanket approach. The blanket scenario (all 441) yields ~Rs 2.3 L but costs 10x more.
Q4 What is the break-even threshold?
Answer: 12.5% churn probability -- this equals the churn model's Bayes threshold. Only customers above this probability should be contacted to expect positive net revenue.
Q5 What should we do?
Answer: Run a controlled win-back test on the top decile (44 customers) with a 20% holdout, measure incremental contribution margin, then decide whether to scale. Use the budget worklist to size the programme.
