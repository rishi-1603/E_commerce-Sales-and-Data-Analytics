# Assumptions & Limitations
### What this analysis can and cannot tell you

> A strong analyst does not pretend the data proves everything. The points
> below are the questions an interviewer will ask — answering them first is a
> sign of maturity, not weakness.

---

## 1. Data limitations
- **The dataset is synthetic.** It is generated by `data/raw/generate_data.py`
  to demonstrate the pipeline. Real e-commerce data has richer, messier
  structure: genuine seasonality, real causal links between discounting and
  behaviour, payment failures, partial returns, and organic customer journeys.
  Treat the specific numbers as **illustrative**, not as measured results.
- **No behavioural/behavioural signals.** There is no clickstream, session,
  cart-abandonment, support-ticket, NPS, or demographic-affluence data — all
  of which materially improve churn and CLV models in practice.
- **3 years of monthly data (36 points).** Adequate for trend + single
  seasonality, but thin for robust multi-season forecasting or deep cohort cuts.

## 2. Correlation ≠ causation (the biggest trap)
Because returns, discounts, channels, and statuses are assigned **independently
at random** in the synthetic data, any relationship between them is noise.
Statements like *"higher discounts cause more returns"* **cannot** be concluded
from this dataset. In a real engagement these would require:
- an A/B test, or
- a quasi-experiment (difference-in-differences, propensity matching), or
- at minimum a controlled regression with confounders.

## 3. Potential bias
- **Survivorship bias:** churn/CLV are estimated only on customers with at
  least one delivered order before the cutoff; brand-new or one-time buyers
  are under-represented.
- **Selection bias in win-back estimates:** customers who respond to win-back
  campaigns are systematically different from those who don't; recovery rates
  must come from a **control group**, not an average.

## 4. Modelling assumptions
- **Churn label horizon (180 days)** is a business choice; results are
  sensitive to it. A 90-day or 365-day horizon would change the churn rate
  and the model's calibration.
- **RFM quintile thresholds** are relative to this dataset; on real data the
  same percentile cut-offs apply but the absolute values differ.
- **Forecast ensemble** assumes the recent trend/seasonality continues; it
  cannot anticipate regime changes (new competitors, pricing shifts, market shocks).

## 5. What CANNOT be concluded from this dataset
- That any product, channel, region, or discount level is *causing* an
  outcome (causation is not identified).
- A reliable financial recovery figure (e.g. "₹29.6L recoverable") — any such
  number requires a control-group test that does not exist here.
- True customer lifetime value (CLV) — only a partial proxy is computed; a
  defensible CLV needs retention/probability-of-alive modelling (e.g. BG/NBD).

## 6. Assumptions we are explicitly making
- Delivered orders are the right basis for revenue/profit (cancelled/returned excluded).
- A 180-day inactivity window is a reasonable proxy for "churned."
- The recent seasonal pattern persists into the forecast horizon.

> **Rule of thumb used throughout:** if a finding could be deleted and no
> business decision would change, it is an observation, not an insight.
